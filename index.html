Research Assistant — National Science and Technology Council

Apr 2026 – present · Advisor Chiao-Ling Kuo

Geospatial text mining over 10.7k+ social-media posts on the Southern Taiwan Science Park — CKIP named-entity recognition and LLM-assisted classification to map public discourse and urban dynamics around high-tech park expansion — alongside digital-twin simulations of the park in NVIDIA OpenUSD.

Read more

The Science Park has reshaped the towns around it, and the argument about that change happens largely online. My work turns that discourse into spatial data: named-entity recognition anchors each post to a place, an anchor-purity test filters the noisy ones out (cutting noise by 22%), and LLM-assisted classification sorts what people are actually debating — transport, infrastructure, wages, public safety.

In parallel, I build digital-twin simulations of the park's expansion in NVIDIA OpenUSD, connecting the discourse layer to the physical one. Together they give the lab a standard pipeline from unstructured text to analyzable geography.

Read more

Did the pandemic change how people work — and did it change differently in different places? Search behavior is the proxy: ~15,000 query series spanning a decade, cleaned, aligned, and tested across Taiwan and four Indian states. I built the pipeline end to end and upgraded its statistical layer from simple ANOVA to Welch tests with false-discovery-rate correction and effect sizes, so the cross-national comparisons hold up under scrutiny. The analysis is co-authored with collaborators in India.

Public Transit Accessibility in Kaohsiung

2023 · 1st Prize, National High School GIS Competition, Taiwan

A PTAL (Public Transport Accessibility Level) analysis of Kaohsiung’s entire public-transport network — some 150 bus routes, the metro and light rail, and 56,129 reference points across all 38 districts — adapted from Transport for London’s methodology and recalibrated to how Kaohsiung actually moves.

Read more

The question began as a personal one: without the metro, could I still get to school? Answering it city-wide meant importing London’s PTAL method and re-tuning every parameter to local reality — peak hour shifted 75 minutes earlier to match Kaohsiung commutes, a 300 m analysis grid in place of London’s 1 km, walking speed recalibrated to 3.6 km/h, and reliability factors set separately for buses and rail from delay studies and operator reports.

The pipeline pulled the full network from OpenStreetMap — 2,308 bus stops, 37 metro and 31 light-rail stations — cleaned directional duplicates in Python, then computed walk time, waiting time, and equivalent doorstep frequency for each of 56,129 grid points in QGIS, interpolated into accessibility maps for every district.

The maps answered more than the original question. Accessibility broadly tracks population density — except in new redevelopment zones, dense but underserved. And comparing the network before and after each rail line quantified their real effect: the metro lifted accessibility scores by 4–5 where it ran; the light rail, only 1.5–2.

NBA Salary Valuation with Machine Learning

2025 · XGBoost · SHAP · D3

An XGBoost system estimating player value as a share of the salary cap, validated on a strict time split (R² = 0.799), with SHAP attribution separating skill from market factors. Served as an interactive D3 dashboard ranking value gaps by player and season.

Read more

Every NBA offseason produces contracts that look wrong; this measures how wrong. The model predicts a player’s share of the salary cap from performance data, validated on a strict time split so it never sees the future. SHAP attribution then splits each valuation into two stories — what a player’s skills justify, and what the market pays for anyway. The results are browsable by player and season in an interactive D3 dashboard.

Smangus Historical Web Map

2025 · Academia Sinica

Century-old Japanese survey maps of the Smangus Atayal community, georeferenced and layered with cultural data gathered in the field — an interactive web map for exploring the settlement across a hundred years of change.

Read more

A hundred years ago, Japanese surveyors mapped the mountains around Smangus; the sheets then sat in archives, unreadable against the modern landscape. Working in the community, I georeferenced the colonial-era maps, aligned them with cultural data gathered in the field, and built a web map where the two centuries sit in the same frame — a tool for the community and for researchers to read change across time.
