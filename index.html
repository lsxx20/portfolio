Research

Research Assistant — National Science and Technology Council

Apr 2026 – present · Advisor Chiao-Ling Kuo

Geospatial text mining over 10,700 forum and social-media posts about the Southern Taiwan Science Park, using CKIP named-entity recognition and LLM-assisted classification to map public discourse and urban dynamics around the park's expansion. Also 3D simulations of the park in NVIDIA OpenUSD.

Read more

Most of the public argument about the park's expansion happens on forums rather than in hearings. The pipeline anchors each post to a place with named-entity recognition, drops the ones whose location is too ambiguous to trust (an anchor-purity test, which cut noise by 22%), then uses LLM-assisted classification to sort posts by what they are actually about: transport, infrastructure, wages, public safety. Classification is the awkward part. Forum text names places inconsistently, and a keyword list gets it wrong often enough to matter.

Separately I build 3D simulations of the park's expansion in NVIDIA OpenUSD. So far these are visualisation rather than modelling, which is the direction I would like to take them next.

Undergraduate Researcher — Geospatial Computing Lab, NTU

Sep 2025 – present · Advisor Tzai-Hung Wen

A spatiotemporal pipeline over roughly 15,000 search-query series (2016–2025), used to quantify post-pandemic shifts in urban work behaviour across Taiwan and four Indian states. Co-authored with collaborators in India.

Read more

Search behaviour is a rough proxy for what people are doing, but it is one of the few things measured consistently across countries for a decade. I built the pipeline end to end over about 15,000 query series, then reworked the statistics: the first version used ANOVA, which was not really appropriate given the unequal variances, so it now runs Welch tests with false-discovery-rate correction and reports effect sizes. The comparative analysis is co-authored with collaborators in India.

Public Transit Accessibility in Kaohsiung

2023 · 1st Prize, National High School GIS Competition, Taiwan

A PTAL (Public Transport Accessibility Level) analysis covering Kaohsiung's whole public-transport network: about 150 bus routes plus metro and light rail, scored at 56,129 reference points across all 38 districts. The method comes from Transport for London, with the parameters recalibrated for Kaohsiung.

Read more

I took the metro to school every day and wanted to know what my commute would look like without it. PTAL turns that into a number, but the London parameters do not transfer directly. Kaohsiung's morning peak runs about 75 minutes earlier, people here walk closer to 3.6 km/h than London's 4.8, and buses are far less punctual than rail, so the reliability factors had to be set separately from delay studies and operator reports. I also used a 300 m grid instead of London's 1 km, which made the maps usable at district level and the computation a great deal slower.

The network came out of OpenStreetMap: 2,308 bus stops, 37 metro stations, 31 light-rail stations. Stops with the same name on opposite sides of a road were being counted twice, so a script removes the duplicates first. After that it is walk time, waiting time, and equivalent doorstep frequency for each of 56,129 grid points in QGIS, interpolated into a map for every district.

Accessibility mostly tracks population density, with one consistent exception: the newer redevelopment districts are dense but score low. Comparing the network before and after each rail line, the metro raised accessibility scores by about 4 to 5 where it ran. The light rail raised them by 1.5 to 2, less than its stated aim of extending the metro's reach would suggest.

NBA Salary Valuation with Machine Learning

2025 · XGBoost · SHAP · D3

An XGBoost system estimating player value as a share of the salary cap, validated on a strict time split (R² = 0.799), with SHAP attribution separating skill from market factors. Published as an interactive D3 dashboard ranking value gaps by player and season.

Read more

The model predicts what share of the salary cap a player should take, from performance data, validated on a time split so it never trains on seasons later than the ones it is tested against. SHAP then splits each prediction into two parts: what the player's production accounts for, and what comes from context like Bird rights, team payroll, and available cap space. Most of the interesting gaps sit in the second part. Results are browsable by player and season in the dashboard.

Smangus Historical Web Map

2025 · Academia Sinica

Century-old Japanese survey maps of the Smangus Atayal community, georeferenced and layered with cultural data collected in the field, as an interactive web map covering a hundred years of change.

Read more

Japanese colonial surveyors mapped this area about a century ago. The sheets survive, but they are difficult to compare against anything current: the control points are gone and many of the place names have changed. I georeferenced them, layered them with cultural data collected with the community, and built a web map that switches between the two periods. It is meant to be usable by the community, not only by researchers.
